What is DAARX?＋

A four-coin system: DAARX for private savings, L10N for private spending, OPEN for public accounts and accountable activity, and Nexus / NXXXS for paid services backed by proof and native collateral.

How can people make money with it?＋

Mining rewards and service fees are the earning mechanisms in the design. Miners receive new coins under the block-reward rules. Service operators can charge for relaying, proofs, execution and other supported work. Holding a coin is a different bet: that people will want to own or use it.

What could make the coins valuable?＋

People wanting them for a reason. DAARX combines private ownership with an issuance schedule that ends. L10N is meant to circulate in payments. OPEN is used for accountable transactions. NXXXS is used for service payments, working balances and collateral. If those uses grow, they can create demand.

Why four separate coins?＋

Saving, spending, public accountability and bonded services have different requirements. Each coin has its own native balances and rules. Shared mining and infrastructure connect them.

What does peak issuance mean?＋

The highest scheduled rate at which new coins enter circulation. DAARX and the NXXXS reference candidate peak near 7.77 target years. OPEN begins at its highest rate. L10N’s current provisional curve declines from its initial reward into a permanent tail.

Who receives newly issued coins?＋

DAARX and L10N have no premine, presale or founder reward in the retained monetary policy. OPEN currently allocates 11% to an endowment and the rest to miners, with its dividend disarmed. NXXXS’s reference candidate has a finite 2% founder stream ending before peak; miners receive the remaining subsidy.

Why would I hold DAARX?＋

For private capital with a deterministic issuance path that eventually stops. That is its intended long-term role in the system.

Why would I use L10N?＋

To make private payments. It is the spending side of the family, with an offline-payment layer being developed alongside the online chain.

Who would use OPEN?＋

People, institutions and agents that need public accounts, defined authority, mandates and receipts. OPEN is broader than a coin for machines.

Who pays for NXXXS services?＋

The design includes wallets, merchants, relayers, proof consumers, solvers, venues, institutions and API providers. Consumers pay for services they choose to use; providers maintain operating balances and commit native collateral.

How does shared mining work?＋

RandomX parent work satisfies the DAARX target and carries L10N, OPEN and NXXXS child commitments. The four chains retain independent native state and rules.

What has already been built?＋

Private work has exercised protocol proofs, transactions, authenticated history, wallet creation, reviewed transfers, encrypted backups and recovery. Shared accounts, games, collectibles and Pride Pot components are also in development.

What is next?＋

Public Testnet. The participant release will bring the software, network coordinates and starter instructions together. Current details are on the Development page.

Is Nexus another coin after NXXXS?＋

They refer to the same fourth lineage. Nexus is the working public identity; NXXXS is the native-unit design. The final public name and ticker are still to be settled.

What is the hardware project?＋

A research catalog of node and mining-system concepts, from compact machines to larger CPU systems, plus a self-build route. The enquiry form records which concepts people would consider.

Who receives a hardware enquiry?＋

Desiderata Labs LLC receives the information through Web3Forms and Proton. The Privacy page explains what is sent, how it is used and how to request access, correction or deletion.
