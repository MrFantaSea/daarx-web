ARCHITECTURE / PEOPLE + INSTITUTIONS + AI

Fairness
by design.

A stronger foundation puts control in the right hands: private lives, bounded permissions, chosen services and visible economic rules. These are the concrete commitments behind the four-coin architecture.

This is the design purpose. Private component and composition tests support parts of it; public deployment and qualification of the complete system remain ahead.

Principle sources: NIST on least privilege; Monero on continuing miner incentives. These explain the principles, not validation or endorsement of DAARX.

01

Privacy for people. Accountability for authority.

DAARX and L10N protect ordinary payment amounts and spend membership. OPEN makes delegated authority explicit through distinct Human, Institution and Agent accounts. The design gives people private financial lives while making permissions and obligations inspectable.

02

Give permission without giving everything.

OPEN mandates put per-transaction, epoch and lifetime limits on Agent spending. Fees consume the allowance, and each ancestor’s budget applies: delegation cannot multiply spending power. Institutions can use threshold authority. This gives people and organizations a concrete way to authorize useful automation while retaining limits.

03

Pay for the service you choose.

Four native roles separate private savings, everyday cash, accountable authority and bonded services. NXXXS service payments go to the selected provider. Ordinary DAARX, L10N and OPEN transfers require no NXXXS purchase or automatic NXXXS fee share. Useful infrastructure earns through chosen work rather than a compulsory toll on sibling activity.

04

Make participation and allocation visible.

Delayed issuance gives participation time to develop beyond launch. DAARX and L10N allocate issuance to miners without a premine, presale or founder reward. OPEN discloses its 11% endowment share; NXXXS discloses its finite 2% founder stream. L10N’s continuing baseline supports miner incentives. These rules let participants inspect who is paid and compare the commitments; practical mining access remains part of the fairness test.

05

Share work. Keep the rules independent.

RandomX parent work carries child commitments, while each chain checks its own target and native validity rules. Shared mining connects the family without merging its balances or monetary policies. The purpose is coordinated infrastructure with distinct responsibilities and validation boundaries.

DAARX

02

L10N

A security budget that continues.

All issuance and fees go to miners. A 1 L10N baseline continues after the finite 77.7-year bonus. This supports miner incentives, while actual security depends on costs, reward value and participation.

03

OPEN

A public endowment allocation.

The current subsidy allocates 11% to the endowment and the rest to miners. The designed dividend is disarmed.

04

NXXXS

A finite founder stream.

The reference candidate assigns 2% of subsidy to the founder stream for roughly seven target years. It ends before peak issuance.

ECONOMICS / FEM-20261106-v1

Opportunity
over time.

The selected model moves L10N and OPEN away from a launch maximum toward year 7.77. DAARX and NXXXS retain their curves. Consensus integration and release qualification remain pending.

01Give participation time to grow.＋

Lower early issuance leaves more distribution for later participants. L10N’s first-year share of its first 25 target years falls from 33.20% to 1.69%; OPEN’s falls from 6.01% to 0.39%. These are gross issuance shares within each version’s 25-year window, not lifetime or ownership percentages. Both cumulative schedules stay at or below their predecessors.

02Different money, different commitments.＋

L10N keeps a permanent 1-coin baseline plus a finite 77.7-year bonus; its supply remains uncapped. OPEN preserves its exact lifetime gross budget of 2,718,281,828,456.627160420 coins with no permanent tail. DAARX issuance ends; NXXXS retains its permanent 0.1 tail. A continuing reward dilutes balances; finite issuance makes future miner income more dependent on fees.

03Judge access as well as the curve.＋

7.77 is symbolic, not proof of fairness. Hardware, electricity, software availability and mining concentration affect who receives coins. Lower launch rewards also reduce early miner income. A nominal floor cannot guarantee security or returns. L10N distributes more than half its finite bonus by peak; DAARX’s later-majority claim does not apply to every curve.

OPEN’s endowment and NXXXS’s finite founder stream concentrate some issuance with named recipients. Transparent percentages make those tradeoffs inspectable; they do not establish equal opportunity.
